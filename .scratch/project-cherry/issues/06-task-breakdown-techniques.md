# Task breakdown techniques that lower the barrier to starting

Type: research
Status: resolved

## Question

What structured techniques and prompts help people with ADHD break a daunting task into startable steps? Cover clinical/coaching sources (CBT for adult ADHD, executive-function coaching), established methods (GTD next physical action, implementation intentions, 'smallest first step'), and how existing tools do it (e.g. Goblin Tools Magic ToDo, Tiimo, Amazing Marvin). Extract concrete guided-question sequences usable in a manual, no-AI breakdown workflow, and common failure modes (over-planning as avoidance, steps still too big).

## Answer

Full findings: [`research/task-breakdown-techniques.md`](https://github.com/Jakeplusplus/project-cherry/blob/research/task-breakdown-techniques/research/task-breakdown-techniques.md) on branch `research/task-breakdown-techniques`.

1. **The one breakdown rule with clinical pedigree is a trigger rule:** "If I am having trouble getting started, then the first step is too big" — a taught self-instruction in Solanto's meta-cognitive therapy (88-person RCT, 2010). The promote-a-too-big-step rule is the same idea. The workflow should detect a non-startable first step and shrink it, not produce a full plan.
2. Both manualised adult-ADHD CBT programmes with RCT support (Safren 2010, Solanto 2010) teach breakdown, but only as one component of a 12-session package. Safren separates "don't know how to proceed" (problem solving) from "too big" (breakdown).
3. **Best wording test for a step:** GTD's "next physical, visible action". Method lore; no outcome trial found.
4. **Plan less than feels natural.** Allen advises against listing sequential steps; Dalton & Spiller (2012) found detailed planning across many goals reduced follow-through (non-ADHD samples). Require one startable step; make the rest optional.
5. **Bucket steps after listing them:** unpacking before estimating reduces the planning fallacy (Kruger & Evans 2004, non-ADHD). A step bucketed long or whole day is the signal to offer promotion.
6. **Existing tools:** Goblin Tools and Tiimo do guided breakdown with AI; manual breakdown elsewhere is a blank subtask list. Only guided non-AI precedent found: Amazing Marvin's Procrastination Wizard, which asks what feels hard before prescribing.
7. **Three candidate sequences** (researcher's synthesis): A "First step only" (recommended default, ends at the timer), B "Lay it out", C "What's in the way?" (diagnose before splitting). Plus a failure-mode table with design responses.

**Biggest caveat:** no study isolates breakdown, and none tests a self-guided or app-delivered version. Evidence is for therapist-delivered multi-component packages, and Cochrane (2018) rates that base as low quality. Sequences, step caps and thresholds are untested — prototype candidates only.

Other limits: implementation-intention evidence in ADHD is children on lab tasks; Goal Management Training showed no advantage in an 81-person adult-ADHD RCT; several sources reached only at abstract or secondary level (tagged in the file); Procrastination Wizard question text not obtained.
